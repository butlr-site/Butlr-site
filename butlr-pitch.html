The problem

Five-star service is broken on the phone.

Every other premium experience in a guest's life moved to their phone a decade ago — Uber, Apple Pay, OpenTable, DoorDash. Hotels didn't. Today's luxury guest still picks up a 1985 phone handset to ask for an espresso.

What's broken

Friction at every touchpoint

Phone-shy younger guests don't dial. Printed menus age out. In-room tablets cost $1,200 each and get ignored. Call-centres go silent at 11pm. Concierges miss requests at scale. Reviews are written in anger because nobody saw the complaint live.

What it costs

Revenue and grace, leaking

8–12% of guest requests never happen because the guest didn't want to call. 15–30% of luxury hotels' ancillary potential is lost to friction. USD 4–5B spent annually on in-room tablets and tech that nobody uses.

The insight

The problem isn't software.
It's the network.

Every venue could build their own app. Most have tried. Adoption is 8–15% because a guest doesn't download an app for a single 3-night stay.

But guests will use an app they already have. The same one. At every venue. That changes everything.

The OpenTable parallel

"Every restaurant could build a reservation system. None do — because diners use OpenTable across restaurants. The same logic locks every venue into Butlr."

The solution

One app. Every venue.
A private butler in every pocket.

Butlr is a white-label, five-star digital butler that lives on the guest's phone and works at every venue in the network. Auto check-in. Digital key. Concierge chat. In-room dining. Spa. Transport. Express checkout.

For hotels: a drag-and-drop back office that replaces in-room tablets, the call centre, the printed menus, and the concierge admin — in three weeks, no development required.
