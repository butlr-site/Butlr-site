Part I

Brand foundation.

The thesis
Every five-star hotel sells the same product: time without friction. Butlr is the smallest, most beautiful object that delivers it.

Butlr is a private butler service that lives on the guest's phone and works in every venue they visit. One app. Every property. Anything they need, summoned in a tap. For hoteliers, Butlr is a white-label butler service, a revenue engine, and a guest-data goldmine — without a single line of code to integrate.

The Butlr guest app is organised around a single principle: the guest never picks up a phone, never calls a desk, never queues. Every action they could take in a hotel is one tap away — and tracked in real time, like a luxury Uber Eats.

Part III

The hotel back office.

If the guest app is the candle on the table, the back office is the kitchen behind the kitchen. A drag-and-drop dashboard the hotel uses to publish menus, route requests, fulfil orders, and track revenue — without writing a line of code. It is to hotels what Talabat's restaurant dashboard is to restaurants, but built for hospitality.

Every property gets a white-label tenant: their logo, their colours, their tone of voice. The guest sees Ritz; we live behind the scenes.

Implementation effort

A new hotel goes live in under three weeks. Week 1: profile + menus loaded. Week 2: PMS and lock integration tested. Week 3: staff training and soft-launch with VIP guests. Full rollout in week 4.

Part IV

Stickiness · the gravitational pull.

A great hotel app is forgotten the moment the guest leaves. Butlr is engineered to be opened between stays. Every feature below is designed to deepen the guest's identity inside Butlr and make the next booking obvious.

Part V

Beyond hotels.

Anywhere a guest expects to be served, Butlr is the layer that makes it feel five-star.

Hotels are the beachhead, not the destination. The Butlr engine — guest verification, service menus, live request routing, staff payouts, concierge chat, and brand-controlled front-end — is the standard for any business where a guest is briefly hosted and expects discretion, speed, and grace.

The unifying thesis

In every vertical above, the same operating logic recurs: an institution hosts a guest briefly; service is expected; the brand cares about how it feels. Butlr is the platform that makes any such institution feel like a five-star hotel, with the same back office, the same Passport, the same Tip Jar, the same Bring-It marketplace.

The guest carries one Butlr through life. The platform changes shape behind each crest.

The exit thesis

Butlr becomes the operating system for premium hospitality globally. The exit options are obvious: a hotel chain (Single-brand A, Accor, Hilton) acquires the network and platform; a payments giant (Stripe, Adyen) acquires the marketplace; a luxury house (LVMH, Richemont) acquires the brand layer.

Whichever, the value is in the network of properties, the loyalty graph of guests, and the proprietary preference data that travels with each one. That graph is what no incumbent can replicate.
